# 15 · Stance carousel (opinion, photo post)

**Pillar:** A · **Length:** 5–7 slides · **Hook type:** Contrarian stance / permission

**Use when:** You have a parenting opinion you'd defend at dinner, a screen-ban story is in the news, or no kid is available to film. TikTok photo mode + IG carousel from the same slides.

## Structure (beats)
[Slide 1] The stance in 8 words or fewer, over a real photo of a kid using the iPad at eye level: 'Screen time is NOT a bad choice.' [Slide 2] The 'because': 'It's a personal choice for your family.' [Slides 3–5] One concrete house rule per slide (no screens at dinner; iPads weekends only; screens come up to eye level). [Slide 6] Where DigiRoo fits, one line, product in the photo. [Last slide] A question parents want to answer.

## Script skeleton
```
1  Screen time is NOT a bad choice.
2  It's a personal choice. Here's what works in our house (10 and 7).
3  Rule 1: no screens at the dinner table.
4  Rule 2: no iPads on school nights.
5  Rule 3: when they do use it, the screen comes up to eye level.
6  That's why I made DigiRoo. (product photo)
7  What's your family's one screen rule? 👇
```

## Shot list skeleton (phone only)
1. Kid using the iPad on the DigiRoo stand at eye level, natural light (cover slide)
2. Dinner table with no devices
3. iPads stacked in a basket / charging drawer
4. Kid upright with the stand raised
5. Product close-up in a real room (not studio)

## On-screen text pattern
Slide 1: one bold line, big type, top third. Other slides: 20 words max, same font and position on every slide.

## Why it works
A stance gives people something to agree with or argue with, and both send it further. Swiping sets its own pace, so people read every slide; on TikTok photo mode it gets pushed to non-followers. It needs no filming day.

## How DigiRoo uses it
One a week while we test (replaces a static graphic). Rotate stances: screen time isn't the enemy; bans backfire; posture over minutes ('It's not just how long, it's how'). Use a trending sound on TikTok. No medical claims (brief §5); the product slide says 'lifts the screen to eye level'.
